Change-of-occupancy checklist

Before a unit is sold, leased, or handed to a new tenant, New Jersey requires a Certificate of Smoke Alarm, Carbon Monoxide Alarm and Portable Fire Extinguisher Compliance (CSACMAPFEC) from your local enforcing agency. The certificate is good for six months or until the next change of occupancy, whichever comes first.

Smoke alarms

  • Ten-year sealed-battery type Standard replaceable-battery units will fail. Sealed 10-year devices are the requirement.
  • One on every level of the unit
  • One outside each separate sleeping area Within 10 feet of the sleeping area.
  • Hardwired units still hardwired You cannot replace an existing hardwired device with a battery one. Common failure.
  • Nothing older than 10 years, all in working order
  • UL/ANSI listed, maintained to NFPA 72

Carbon monoxide alarms

  • Within 10 feet of each sleeping area
  • Working per manufacturer's spec Battery, plug-in, or part of a central station system are all acceptable.

Portable fire extinguisher

  • Listed, labeled, charged and operable
  • Brand new with receipt, or tagged within the last 12 months Tag must be from a Division of Fire Safety certified contractor. An untagged old extinguisher fails.
  • Mounted and readily accessible If it's in a cabinet or closet, the door must be labeled "Fire Extinguisher Inside."
  • In or within 10 feet of the kitchen
  • Top no more than 5 feet off the floor Bottom at least 4 inches up. Chest height is the safe target.
  • Owner's manual left for the new occupant

If the unit has a fire alarm system

  • Tested before the inspection by a certified alarm contractor
  • Test record in hand for the inspector Inspectors generally will not test the system themselves. No report usually means an automatic fail.
Apply early. Municipal fees climb sharply the closer you get to the occupancy date — the same certificate can run roughly $40–$70 with over ten business days' notice and $125–$211 inside four days. Fees are set locally, so confirm with your town.

What has to be posted in the building

Multiple dwellings — three or more units — must post emergency contact information and social services hotline instructions in a conspicuous spot where tenants will see them, in both English and Spanish. Management companies must also put this on their website.

Also required

  • Registration statement posted where it's easily seen Buildings with three or more units file with the DCA Bureau of Housing Inspection. One- and two-family non-owner-occupied houses file with the municipal clerk. A copy also goes to the tenant.
  • Truth in Renting statement given to every tenant At or before the time occupancy begins. Free from the DCA website.
  • Emergency special-assistance list notice Tell tenants the municipal clerk keeps a list of residents who need help evacuating in an emergency, and how to get on it.
  • Flood zone disclosure in the lease, if applicable
  • Website address and hotline instructions in boldface in every lease

Window guard acknowledgment

New Jersey requires more than a line in the lease. At signing you must verbally tell the tenant they can request window guards, and that has to be confirmed in a written document separate from the lease, signed by both parties. The lease itself also needs the notice in prominent boldface.

Lead-safe certification

Rental units built before 1978 need a visual inspection or dust wipe test, then re-inspection every three years or at tenant turnover, whichever comes first. Lead-safe certificates are valid two years.

The exemption most landlords don't know about

Multiple dwellings registered with DCA for at least ten years with no outstanding lead violations from the most recent cyclical inspection under the Hotel and Multiple Dwelling Law are exempt from this requirement. If you have a current certificate of inspection from the Bureau of Housing Inspection, check whether it covers you before paying for a separate lead inspection.

  • Built 1978 or later — exempt, no inspection needed
  • Certified lead-free by a certified evaluation contractor — exempt
  • Registered with DCA 10+ years, no outstanding lead violations — likely exempt
  • Otherwise: schedule inspection at turnover or every 3 years
  • Certificate filed with the municipality if you hired privately
If hazards are found you'll need either interim controls — cleaning, repair, repainting, containment — or full abatement. Income-eligible units may qualify for free work through the state's Lead Remediation and Abatement Program.

TriggerWhat's required
Every turnoverCSACMAPFEC certificate — smoke, CO and extinguisher inspection. Apply 10+ business days ahead to avoid rush fees.
