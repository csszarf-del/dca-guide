The January notice cycle, tracked properly

Lead paint and window guard notices go out the first two weeks of January. What trips owners up isn't sending them — it's what happens to the ones that never come back. Miss that step and you're liable, whether or not a child lives there.

January 1–15

Send the annual notice to every unit

Multiple dwellings with 3+ units. First-class mail, hand delivery, or enclosed with the January rent bill if that bill goes out between December 15 and January 16. Lead and window guard notices can go on one combined form. Where lead applies, provide duplicates in English and Spanish so the tenant keeps one and returns one.

By February 15

Tenants return the form

It asks whether a child lives in the unit. Two different ages matter: 10 or younger triggers window guards, 5 or younger triggers lead paint inspection and safe work practices.

February 16 – March 1

Inspect every unit that didn't respond

No response means you go look. Document every attempt to reach the tenant.

By March 1

Write to the Health Department for units you couldn't enter

If a tenant refused access or you simply couldn't get in, describe the situation in a letter to DOHMH. That letter is what protects you.

Keep for 10 years

File every returned form

Ten-year retention. Lead compliance is also certified as part of your annual property registration.

The part people miss. A unit that never returns the form is not resolved. You must inspect it, and if you can't get in, you must write to the Health Department by March 1. Skipping that step leaves you exposed if a child is later injured — and it's the first thing asked about after a window fall.
Window guards are required in any unit where a child 10 or younger lives, and on tenant request whether or not a child lives there. A tenant can ask any time of year, not just in January.
