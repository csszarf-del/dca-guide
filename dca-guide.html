I

The clock is always running

You don't schedule this inspection — the State comes to you. Here's the exact timeline once they do, and where owners get burned.

Day 0

The inspection happens

A state inspector walks your common areas and units — including the owner's unit if you live there. You can see your inspection coming on the DCA Service Portal, so check it before they show up.

Report mailed + 10 days

Your 60-day clock starts

You get 60 days to correct violations — and the clock starts 10 days after the Bureau mails your inspection report by regular mail.

After day 60

Reinspection

The inspector returns to check your corrections. If they can't get into the building after three attempts, you get 14 days to schedule — or you're penalized. Once your work is done, you can request an early reinspection in writing.

The result

Your building gets tiered

Pass by the first reinspection: 7-year cycle. Pass by the second or third: 5 years. Still failing: 2-year cycle, with inspection fees due every time. Then, once everything is corrected and paid, you download your Certificate of Inspection — the "Green Card" — from the portal.

II

Deadlines you can't miss

Three of these protect your right to fight a violation or buy more time — miss the window and that right is gone. The fourth just costs you money outright.

15 days

To request a hearing

Want to contest violations? Your Administrative Hearing request must be postmarked within 15 days of the orders being served — and any issue you don't raise is waived forever.

30 days

To request an exception

Think a specific violation shouldn't apply to your building? File the state's Application for Exception form within 30 days of the report being served.

2 weeks

To request an extension

Need more time to fix things? Extension requests must go in at least 2 weeks before the reinspection date printed on your report — with what's done, what's not, and when it will be.

$200+

For missing recertification

Every owner must recertify their registration annually ($25/building, within 60 days of expiration). Miss it and a $200 judgment is entered — with new judgments every 30 days after.

V

Got cited? You have three plays

A violation isn't the end of the conversation. New Jersey gives owners real options — but every one of them is on a clock.

Play 1

Request an Exception

⏱ 30 days from service

If you believe a specific cited violation shouldn't apply to your building, file the State's official Application for Exception form in writing within 30 days.

Get the exception form →
Play 2

Demand a Hearing

⏱ 15 days — issues not raised are waived

To fully contest violations, request an Administrative Hearing through the DCA portal or in writing. Include every issue — factual, legal, or procedural — because anything left out is waived. This is where a violations attorney earns their fee. Ask us for a referral.

DCA Service Portal →
Play 3

Negotiate the Penalty

⏱ Call as soon as you're penalized

Penalties for unpaid inspection fees can't be reduced — but penalties for failure to correct violations CAN be negotiated with the Bureau's Compliance Section. Most owners never know to ask.

Call Compliance: (609) 633-6229 →
