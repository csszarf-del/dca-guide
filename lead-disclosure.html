The lead disclosure every pre-1978 rental needs

This is the federal form under 40 CFR Part 745 and 24 CFR Part 35. It's the one Newark Housing Authority, NYCHA and HPD want in your Section 8 packet — but it isn't a housing authority form. Every landlord renting a pre-1978 unit owes it to every tenant, voucher or not, before the lease is signed.

Filling a Section 8 packet? The PHA will reject an RFTA without this form attached for any pre-1978 unit. Complete your parts, then have the tenant sign before you submit.
Two things go with this form, not just the form. You must also give the tenant the EPA pamphlet Protect Your Family from Lead in Your Home, and you must keep the signed disclosure for at least three years. A signed form with no pamphlet is still a violation.

Disclosure of Information on Lead-Based Paint
and Lead-Based Paint Hazards

Required for housing built before 1978 · 42 U.S.C. 4852d · 40 CFR Part 745 · 24 CFR Part 35

Property Address:  

Lead Warning Statement. Housing built before 1978 may contain lead-based paint. Lead from paint, paint chips, and dust can pose health hazards if not managed properly. Lead exposure is especially harmful to young children and pregnant women. Before renting pre-1978 housing, lessors must disclose the presence of known lead-based paint and/or lead-based paint hazards in the dwelling. Lessees must also receive a federally approved pamphlet on lead poisoning prevention.

What else your city wants

State rules are only half of it. These are the local requirements landlords in our area get caught by.

Newark — rental registration. Every rented unit in the city must be registered with the Department of Economic and Housing Development, and you cannot lease a unit without a Certificate of Habitability. The city inspects each rental at least once every three years or on a change of occupancy. Violations run up to $1,250 each. Registration stays valid as long as the occupancy and the underlying information don't change.
Newark — rent control registration, separate from the above. All dwelling units register annually with the Rent Control Board: $50 per year plus $10 per unit. Late runs $100 plus $20 per unit for every year past due. Allowable annual increases are capped at 4%, and you lose the increase entirely if the building isn't in substantial compliance or you haven't registered. No registration and no taxes current means no Certificate of Habitability.
Jersey City — landlord registration. Fewer than three rental units and non-owner-occupied: file the Landlord Registration Form with the City Clerk. More than two units: file with the NJ DCA Bureau of Housing Inspection instead, and DCA forwards a copy to the City Clerk. Owner-occupied is excepted.
Where landlords lose money. In Newark, the rent control registration and the rental property registration are two different filings with two different offices. Owners routinely do one and assume they're covered — then find out the missed one blocks their annual increase or their Certificate of Habitability.
